MAE, MSE, and R² Score in Linear Regression
--------------------------------------------------

- When a Linear Regression model predicts values, we need a way to measure how good or bad the predictions are.
- That's where MAE, MSE, and R² Score come in.
- Think of them as a report card for your model.

MAE = "How wrong am I on average?"
MSE = "Did I make any very big mistakes?"

1. MAE (Mean Absolute Error)
------------------------------

MAE tells us:

"On average, how many marks is the model wrong by?"

2. MSE (Mean Squared Error)
--------------------------------

MSE tells us:

"How large are the errors, giving extra punishment to bigger mistakes?"
" MSE helps us identify and heavily punish large mistakes."

Why do we need MSE? (Simple Explanation)
-----------------------------------------


3. R² Score (R-Squared)
-----------------------

R² measures:

"How much of the variation in data is explained by the model?"

Instead of measuring error directly, it measures goodness of fit.

R² Score   	 Meaning
1.0		Perfect prediction
0.9		Very good
0.8		Good
0.5		Moderate
0.0		No predictive power
Less than 0	Worse than predicting the average


Why Do We Need All Three?
--------------------------

Each metric tells a different story.

Metric		What It Answers
MAE		How wrong are predictions on average?
MSE		Are there any large mistakes?
R²		How well does the model fit the data?

Think of hiring a cricket player:

MAE = Average runs missed.
MSE = Punishes huge failures.
R² = Overall performance rating.
